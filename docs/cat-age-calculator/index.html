How the calculation works

People ask for a single “cat year to human year” number because it makes life stages easier to talk about. Cats do not age on a human calendar, but a transparent chart still helps owners notice when kitten care should give way to adult maintenance, and when senior monitoring becomes more important.

CatCalc uses a widely shared informal approximation: the first year counts as about 15 human years, the second year brings the total to about 24, and each year after that adds about 4 human years. Ages under one year are interpolated from typical kitten development so a 3-month-old kitten does not show the same result as a 1-year-old.

This is not a clinical geriatric assessment, not a dental aging method, and not an AAHA/AAFP–endorsed conversion formula. The life-stage labels we show beside the result come from a separate source—the 2021 AAHA/AAFP feline life stage definitions—so we do not pretend the arithmetic and the guidelines are one document.

Worked examples (same math as the calculator)

These match the live JavaScript on this page. Try the same inputs to verify.

  • 0 years, 8 months: age under 1 year uses the kitten interpolation table. At 8 months the script returns about 12 human years, life stage Kitten.
  • 2 years, 0 months: after year two the chart is 24 human years → about 24 human years, life stage Young adult.
  • 12 years, 0 months: 24 + 4×(12−2) = 64 human years, life stage Senior. Use that label as a prompt for more frequent exams if your vet agrees—not as a diagnosis.

Empty fields, non-numbers, years outside 0–35, or months outside 0–11 show an inline error and do not invent a result. Months must be whole numbers.

Life stages in plain language

Kitten (to 1 year): rapid growth, vaccine series planning, diet labeled for growth or all life stages. Young adult (1–6): maintain healthy weight and dental care. Mature adult (7–10): watch for subtle weight or thirst changes. Senior (over 10): more frequent wellness checks are commonly recommended; see our senior weight guide.

What this tool does not do

It does not read bloodwork, estimate remaining lifespan for an individual cat, or replace a veterinary exam. Breed, outdoor access, dental disease and medical conditions all matter more than any viral chart. If your cat’s behavior or appetite changes, call your clinic—do not wait for a round birthday on this page.

FAQ

How old is my cat in human years?

This informal approximation uses about 15 human years at age 1, 24 at age 2, then 4 more per cat year. It is not a clinical measurement or an endorsed AAHA/AAFP formula.

What are the life stages of a cat?

Kitten: birth to 1 year. Young adult: 1-6 years. Mature adult: 7-10 years. Senior: over 10 years (2021 AAHA/AAFP Feline Life Stage Guidelines).

How long do indoor cats live?

Many indoor cats live into their mid-teens, and some reach 20 or more. Regular vet checks become more important from the senior stage.

Why does my kitten's age jump so fast in human years?

Cats mature quickly in the first two years. The chart front-loads that development, then uses a steadier four-human-year step. It is a teaching aid, not a biological clock.

Should I change food when the calculator says "senior"?

Life stage is a reminder to talk with your vet about exam frequency and diet. Do not switch medical or senior diets based only on an online chart.
