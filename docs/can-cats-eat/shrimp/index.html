Can cats eat cooked shrimp?

By CatCalc editorial team · Updated 2026-11-12 · Not veterinary advice

Small treat · cooked

Plain cooked shrimp can be a small treat with the shell and tail removed.

Why

Plain cooked shrimp can be an occasional reward if suitable for the cat. Shells, tails, seasoning and the overall diet need separate checks; shrimp is not a complete meal.

Shrimp vary in size, so a universal shrimp-count serving is misleading. Keep garlic butter, cocktail sauce, brine and fried coatings out of the cat's portion.

The shrimp name does not describe the dish

A shrimp taken from a garlic-butter pan is a different food from a shrimp cooked alone. Restaurant scampi, fried shrimp, cocktail platters and ready-to-eat salads can introduce ingredients beyond the seafood itself. Before sharing, identify how the food was cooked and what touched it, including sauces served underneath or alongside it.

ASPCA identifies onion and garlic as causes of red blood cell damage in cats. Removing a visible garlic piece does not establish that the shrimp is plain. If a seasoned dish was eaten, keep the recipe or package and consult the garlic page for the information the veterinary team needs. For a planned reward, a separate unseasoned piece is simpler than trying to rinse a finished human dish into suitability.

Be particularly clear about ready-cooked products: already cooked describes heat treatment, while plain describes ingredients. A chilled shrimp from a deli platter may satisfy the first description without satisfying the second. Keep the package until the reward is finished, and distinguish the shrimp from the dipping sauce when explaining exactly what the cat could have eaten.

Cook the flesh and discard the shell before portioning

Use cooked shrimp, remove the head, shell and tail, and cut the flesh into small manageable pieces. This is a practical preparation approach rather than a validated feline serving protocol. Do not give the cat a hard tail to chew while the rest of dinner is prepared. The shell and tail are not useful nutritional additions that need to be preserved.

FDA’s food-handling guidance describes cooked shrimp flesh as pearly and opaque. Follow package cooking instructions and safe storage practices, and let the cat’s portion cool. A pink color alone is not the entire ingredient or handling history of a prepared product. Raw seafood raises a separate pathogen question: CDC advises against raw animal treats because they can carry harmful germs. See raw-fish guidance for the broader distinction between cooked seafood and raw feeding.

A small seafood reward is not a mineral supplement

Shrimp can be appealing without being required for a balanced feline diet. Do not assume that trace nutrients in an ingredient make it a useful treatment for a deficiency or a reason to add it every day. Cornell explains that cats need a carefully balanced range of nutrients and that more vitamins or minerals are not necessarily better. A complete food already aims to supply the required balance.

Likewise, shrimp flesh alone is not a complete meal simply because it is animal protein. FDA distinguishes complete-and-balanced pet food from treats and supplementary foods. If you want a seafood-based staple, assess the cat food’s adequacy statement instead of assembling a bowl from plain shellfish. The cat-food label guide helps locate the relevant wording. Avoid replacing complete meals with shrimp when the cat becomes enthusiastic about its smell.

Use measured extras rather than a shrimp-count rule

Shrimp vary greatly in size, so “one shrimp” is a poor universal portion description. Cut the reward before offering it and count its energy with the day’s other extras. A kitchen scale and relevant product information are more useful for budgeting than a photograph of a serving intended for another cat.

WSAVA recommends that all treats together supply no more than 10% of daily calories. The calorie calculator estimates the daily total from weight and a life-stage factor, but the veterinarian’s target takes priority. A calorie ceiling does not establish a safe shrimp dose or override a medical feeding plan. Compare cooked salmon and tuna as other optional seafoods with their own preparation concerns. There is no need to rotate through all of them to make a complete feline diet more complete.

After a swallowed tail or an unexplained reaction

Note whether shell, tail, packaging or a skewer may be missing, and describe any sauce. Gagging, trouble swallowing or abnormal breathing after eating requires urgent veterinary attention; do not try to push a suspected foreign object onward with more food. If the cat seems uncomfortable, contact the clinic rather than waiting for the next planned meal.

For digestive illness, Cornell advises prompt evaluation when vomiting comes with weakness, lethargy, reduced appetite, blood or diarrhea. Keep a record of the timing and amount without deciding that every reaction must be a seafood allergy. Your veterinarian can assess the possible causes and decide what to avoid next. Once a cat has reacted poorly to a particular food, continuing increasingly small taste tests is less useful than choosing a reward already known to suit its feeding plan.

How to serve it

Serve only a small cooled piece of fully cooked, unseasoned shrimp with head, shell and tail removed.

Treats and complete diets

If this food is appropriate at all, keep it inside the daily calorie budget. Use the calorie calculator and keep treats near or under about 10% of total kcal. Human food is not a balanced cat diet.
