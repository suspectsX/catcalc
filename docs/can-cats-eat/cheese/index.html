Can cats eat cheese?

By CatCalc editorial team · Updated 2026-11-02 · Not veterinary advice

Caution · fat and salt

Only a rare tiny piece if tolerated; cheese may cause digestive upset.

Why

Cheese has less lactose than milk, but it is high in fat and salt. A tiny nibble may be tolerated; larger amounts may cause tummy trouble.

Lactose is lower than in milk, but fat and salt are not free. Soft cheese, hard cheese and cheese sauces differ; sauces often hide onion or garlic. Medication hiding in cheese should be vet-approved for that drug and that cat. A pea-sized nibble is the usual outer bound people discuss—not a cheese board.

Plain cheese vs sauces

A pea-sized bit of plain cheese is what people usually mean by “a nibble.” Cheese sauces, nacho cheese and seasoned soft cheeses often include onion, garlic or heavy salt.

Fat, salt and medication tricks

Lower lactose than milk does not make cheese a free food. High fat can upset some cats. Hiding pills in cheese is only appropriate when your vet agrees for that drug and that patient.

Compared with milk and yogurt

Milk is usually worse for lactose; yogurt must be plain and unsweetened if used at all; cheese concentrates fat and salt. None of these replace a complete cat diet.

If offering cheese

If tolerated, keep it as a rare, tiny treat. Ask your vet before using food to give medication.

Treats and complete diets

If this food is appropriate at all, keep it inside the daily calorie budget. Use the calorie calculator and keep treats near or under about 10% of total kcal. Human food is not a balanced cat diet.
