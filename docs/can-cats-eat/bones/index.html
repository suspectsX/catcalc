Can cats eat cooked bones?

By CatCalc editorial team · Updated 2026-11-02 · Not veterinary advice

Do not feed

Do not feed cooked bones: they can splinter, choke or injure a cat.

If your cat already ate this

  1. Contact your veterinarian or an emergency clinic now—do not wait for symptoms.
  2. In the US you can also call ASPCA Animal Poison Control: 888-426-4435 or Pet Poison Helpline: 855-764-7661 (fees may apply).
  3. Have ready: approximate amount and time, product packaging or ingredients, your cat’s weight, and any symptoms.
  4. Do not induce vomiting or give home remedies unless a veterinarian specifically instructs you to.

Why

Cooked bones become brittle and can splinter, causing choking or injuries to the mouth and gut. Raw bones also carry injury and infection risks.

Cooked bones become brittle and can splinter, causing choking or injuries to the mouth, throat or gut. Raw bones still carry fracture and infection risks and are not a “safer leftover.” If a bone is stuck or the cat struggles to breathe, that is emergency care—not an internet search.

Why cooked bones splinter

Cooking makes bones brittle. Splinters can choke a cat or injure the mouth, throat or intestines. Leftover chicken legs, turkey carcasses and rib bones from human plates are common sources.

What to do after swallowing

Contact your veterinarian promptly. Choking, pawing at the mouth, retching, drooling or breathing difficulty are emergency signs—go to urgent care rather than waiting for an online answer.

Raw bones are not a free pass

Raw bones still risk tooth fractures, obstruction and bacterial contamination. CatCalc does not recommend leftover bones as chew toys or calcium supplements without veterinary guidance.

What to do

Do not give cooked bones. If swallowed, contact your vet promptly; choking or breathing difficulty requires emergency care.
