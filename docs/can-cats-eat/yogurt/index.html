Can cats eat yogurt?

By CatCalc editorial team · Updated 2026-11-02 · Not veterinary advice

Caution · plain only

A small amount of plain unsweetened yogurt may be tolerated; check ingredients.

Why

Plain, unsweetened yogurt is lower in lactose than milk and some cats tolerate a small spoonful. Avoid flavored yogurts and any containing xylitol or chocolate.

Fruit-on-the-bottom cups, chocolate swirls, honey toppings and sugar-free products with polyols are the usual traps. Plain, unsweetened yogurt with no xylitol is the only version worth discussing—and even then only as a tiny optional spoonful if your cat tolerates dairy. Yogurt is not a probiotic prescription; gut problems need a vet.

Plain unsweetened only

If yogurt is offered at all, choose plain and unsweetened with no xylitol. Fruit-on-the-bottom cups, chocolate swirls and honey toppings add sugar or other hazards. Start with a teaspoon only if your cat already tolerates dairy.

Lactose and probiotics caveats

Yogurt is often lower in lactose than milk, but it is not automatically gentle. It is also not a substitute for veterinary care when diarrhea or constipation persists. Do not treat yogurt as prescribed probiotic therapy.

Compared with milk and cheese

Cow’s milk is usually worse for lactose. Cheese concentrates fat and salt. None of these dairy options replace a complete cat diet.

If offering yogurt

Start with a teaspoon and watch for diarrhea.

Treats and complete diets

If this food is appropriate at all, keep it inside the daily calorie budget. Use the calorie calculator and keep treats near or under about 10% of total kcal. Human food is not a balanced cat diet.
