Can cats eat raw eggs?

By CatCalc editorial team · Updated 2026-11-12 · Not veterinary advice

Cook first

Do not serve raw eggs. Cook eggs fully without seasoning before sharing.

Why

Raw eggs can carry harmful bacteria. Avidin in raw egg white binds biotin and can interfere with its absorption, particularly with repeated feeding in an unbalanced diet.

Avidin binds biotin; it is not a claim that a single raw-egg lick causes an immediate deficiency. Cooking and a complete feline diet address different parts of the feeding question.

The shell does not certify a sterile breakfast

An intact-looking egg is still an uncooked animal food. Cracking it into a clean bowl does not establish that harmful bacteria are absent. ASPCA warns that raw eggs can contain Salmonella or E. coli that can harm pets and people. A cat does not need to appear ill immediately for the household’s handling practices to matter; raw residues on utensils and surfaces are another exposure route.

Do not let the cat lick the bowl used for cake batter or mayonnaise. Those mixtures can introduce other ingredients in addition to raw egg. Keep preparation bowls away from pets, clean the counter and wash hands after handling eggs. The appropriate alternative, when an egg reward fits the cat’s diet, is a separately cooked plain portion described on our cooked-egg page.

Do not leave a cracked egg or batter bowl unattended while preheating the oven. Put shells straight into a covered waste container and keep the cat off the preparation surface. If another person finishes the recipe, explain which utensils contacted raw egg so that cleaning does not depend on remembering where a bowl happened to stand.

Avidin binds a vitamin; it is not instant poisoning

Raw egg white contains avidin, a protein that binds biotin and can interfere with its absorption. Merck Veterinary Manual explains this interaction and the role of cooking in reducing antinutritional factors. The concern is especially relevant to repeated feeding in an unbalanced diet. It should not be exaggerated into a claim that one small lick immediately strips vitamins from the cat’s body.

Egg yolk contains nutrients, but feeding yolks while discarding whites is not a complete nutritional solution. It does not create a balanced feline menu, and a raw yolk remains a raw animal-food exposure. Likewise, a biotin supplement does not resolve the microbial question. If someone has been routinely adding raw eggs to the bowl, bring the whole diet history to the veterinarian instead of trying to repair a single nutrient in isolation.

Soft yolks, pasteurized products and cooking

For simple home sharing, fully cooked egg is easier to assess than an uncooked mixture. FDA advises cooking eggs until both white and yolk are firm, with egg dishes reaching 160°F. Cool the portion and keep it plain. Runny breakfast eggs, a raw egg cracked onto kibble and a fully set boiled egg are different preparations even though all began in the same carton.

Pasteurization is a processing claim, not proof that an egg product is a complete diet or suitable in unlimited quantities. Check the package and follow its directions; do not infer that all liquid egg products have identical ingredients. Milk, cheese, cooking fat, onion and seasoning can be added before or after heating. Our milk and onion pages explain why those additions require their own assessment.

Why an egg is an extra rather than a nutritional fix

A raw egg is sometimes proposed for a shinier coat or to compensate for a low-quality diet. That substitutes a kitchen habit for checking whether the main food is nutritionally adequate. FDA distinguishes complete-and-balanced foods from treats and supplements; the right question is whether the staple meets the cat’s life-stage needs, not whether another ingredient sounds nutritious.

If cooked egg is allowed as a reward, count it with the day’s other extras. WSAVA limits treats to no more than 10% of daily calories. Use the calorie calculator as a starting estimate and the actual egg preparation’s energy information where available. Do not copy a whole-egg serving from a human breakfast plan. An energy allowance is not a validated safe egg dose or permission to bypass a medical diet.

A useful response to a stolen lick

Remove the raw mixture and identify everything it contained. Plain egg white, chocolate cake batter and a bowl of seasoned ground meat call for different advice. Keep the ingredient labels and note the time and approximate amount. Contact your veterinarian if a potentially toxic co-ingredient was involved; do not wait for the raw-egg question to be settled first.

For illness after any exposure, Cornell recommends prompt evaluation when vomiting accompanies weakness, lethargy, reduced appetite, blood or diarrhea. A veterinarian can decide what monitoring or testing is appropriate for the individual cat. Do not give antibiotics, vitamin doses or human stomach remedies as a precaution. The cat-food label guide offers a better route for long-term nutrition: verify the regular diet and discuss changes with the clinic rather than adding raw ingredients to it.

Cook before serving

Avoid raw eggs. Any occasional egg reward should be fully cooked and plain, without milk, butter or seasoning.

Treats and complete diets

If this food is appropriate at all, keep it inside the daily calorie budget. Use the calorie calculator and keep treats near or under about 10% of total kcal. Human food is not a balanced cat diet.
