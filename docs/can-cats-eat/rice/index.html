Can cats eat rice?

By CatCalc editorial team · Updated 2026-11-12 · Not veterinary advice

Small treat · cooked

Only a little plain cooked rice; it cannot replace a complete cat diet.

Why

Cats can use properly cooked rice, but rice is not complete nutrition or a universal diarrhea treatment. A veterinarian should choose the diet for an unwell cat.

Distinguish a few plain grains from a repeated rice-based ration. Check broth and sauces, and do not assume a homemade chicken-and-rice diet is complete.

Edible starch is not a complete feline meal

Plain cooked rice is not the same thing as a rice-based feeding plan. The 2022 ISFM feline nutrition guidance explains that cats can digest and use appropriately processed starch, while still having requirements that starch cannot meet by itself. Calling cats carnivores does not mean every cooked grain is poisonous. Calling rice digestible does not mean it can replace a balanced diet.

If a healthy cat steals a few grains from a plate, identify whether they were truly plain before deciding what the exposure involved. A substantial portion of rice served repeatedly is a different concern because of what it may displace. Our food-label guide helps distinguish a complete product from a complementary food. A bowl of white rice remains an ingredient even when it is soft, familiar and easy to serve.

Why the chicken-and-rice shortcut needs a second look

Rice is commonly mentioned when a cat has diarrhea, but the familiar name of a home recipe does not identify the cause or the right diet. VCA's guidance on bland diets explains that chicken and rice is nutritionally incomplete and that more appropriate formulated gastrointestinal diets are available. This is why we do not publish a universal chicken-to-rice ratio or instruct owners to keep a sick cat on it indefinitely.

If your veterinarian says to use a bland diet, ask which exact food, what measured daily amount and for how long. The word bland can describe very different feeding intentions. Keep track of what the cat actually eats, rather than assuming a full-looking bowl means its needs were met. If it rejects the agreed food, report that promptly instead of silently replacing it with rice alone or a succession of human leftovers.

Plain cooking means no stock cube

For an optional taste, use well-cooked rice prepared in plain water and cooled to a comfortable temperature. Do not count a broth-cooked pot as plain without checking the broth. Stock, onion powder and garlic can be easy to overlook because the finished grains do not look seasoned. ASPCA identifies onion and garlic among foods to avoid; our garlic page explains why small-looking powder additions matter.

Fried rice, risotto, pilaf, rice pudding and sushi are separate dishes with their own ingredients. Scraping off a visible topping cannot reconstruct a plain portion. If you want to reserve some, do so before sauces or additions enter the pan. Uncooked dry rice is not the preparation discussed here and offers no advantage as a snack. Keep dry goods and opened packets away from a cat that chews packaging.

Measure the cooked food, not an imagined serving

Rice absorbs water during cooking, so a spoon of dry grains and a spoon of cooked rice are not interchangeable measures for energy planning. If a veterinarian has specified a weighed addition, clarify whether the weight refers to cooked or uncooked product and follow that instruction consistently. Do not use a generic cup description to decide how much normal cat food to remove.

The calorie calculator estimates overall daily energy from body weight and a selected factor. It cannot supply the energy density of your pot or confirm nutritional adequacy. An optional rice taste belongs with other extras, not in a separate unrestricted category. If the cat has a medical feeding plan, ask whether any rice fits it before offering it. The oatmeal page makes a similar distinction for another cooked grain whose preparation changes the weight and texture substantially.

Use symptoms to seek help, not to choose the next home remedy

Repeated vomiting, blood in stool, marked lethargy, pain or poor eating are reasons to contact a veterinarian rather than simply increasing rice. VCA's feline diarrhea information describes diarrhea as a sign with multiple possible causes. A normal-looking stool after a home meal also does not prove that a suspected toxin, foreign object or underlying disease has been dealt with.

Save the recipe or packaging after an accidental meal and tell the clinic about raw ingredients, sauce and any non-food material that could have been swallowed. Keep the history brief but complete: what was eaten, approximately how much, when, and what changed afterward. Do not add human anti-diarrhea medicine or begin prolonged fasting on the basis of an online rice suggestion. The feeding instructions should match the cat's age, condition and actual assessment, not the reputation of a kitchen staple.

How to serve it

Offer only a little plain well-cooked rice if appropriate. For a symptom-directed diet, use the exact food and amount recommended by your veterinarian.

Treats and complete diets

If this food is appropriate at all, keep it inside the daily calorie budget. Use the calorie calculator and keep treats near or under about 10% of total kcal. Human food is not a balanced cat diet.
