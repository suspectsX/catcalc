Can cats eat garlic?

By CatCalc editorial team · Updated 2026-11-02 · Not veterinary advice

Do not feed

Garlic can damage red blood cells; avoid it in all forms.

If your cat already ate this

  1. Contact your veterinarian or an emergency clinic now—do not wait for symptoms.
  2. In the US you can also call ASPCA Animal Poison Control: 888-426-4435 or Pet Poison Helpline: 855-764-7661 (fees may apply).
  3. Have ready: approximate amount and time, product packaging or ingredients, your cat’s weight, and any symptoms.
  4. Do not induce vomiting or give home remedies unless a veterinarian specifically instructs you to.

Why

Garlic is an Allium like onions and is considered even more potent. Garlic powder in broths, baby food and leftovers is a common hidden source.

Garlic salt, garlic oil, garlic powder and savory broth concentrates are common hidden sources. Garlic is often described as more potent than onion on a weight basis among Allium foods, which is why leftover takeout and seasoned meats are frequent accidental exposures. Treat any intentional sharing of garlic-seasoned food as a vet call, not a home experiment.

Hidden garlic in human food

Garlic powder, garlic salt, garlic oil, and “savory” broths show up in rotisserie seasoning, noodle cups and leftovers. A cat that licks a garlic-butter plate is an exposure even if no clove was offered on purpose.

What to tell the vet

Describe the dish, rough amount, time, and any vomiting or lethargy. Bring labels when garlic is one of many ingredients. Do not wait days “to see” if the portion seemed small.

Compared with onions

Both are Allium plants linked to red blood cell injury in cats. Onion pages and garlic pages exist separately so searchers find either name; the action after ingestion is the same: professional advice, not a homemade dose chart.

What to do

Contact your vet, and check labels of any human food or broth you share with your cat.

Sources and limits

  • ASPCA: people foods to avoid
  • Pet Poison Helpline: onion (Allium family)
