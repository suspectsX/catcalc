Can cats eat chocolate?

By CatCalc editorial team · Updated 2026-11-02 · Not veterinary advice

Do not feed

Chocolate contains toxic methylxanthines; contact your vet after ingestion.

If your cat already ate this

  1. Contact your veterinarian or an emergency clinic now—do not wait for symptoms.
  2. In the US you can also call ASPCA Animal Poison Control: 888-426-4435 or Pet Poison Helpline: 855-764-7661 (fees may apply).
  3. Have ready: approximate amount and time, product packaging or ingredients, your cat’s weight, and any symptoms.
  4. Do not induce vomiting or give home remedies unless a veterinarian specifically instructs you to.

Why

Chocolate contains theobromine and caffeine, which cats cannot break down well. Even small amounts can cause vomiting, diarrhea, a racing heart, tremors and seizures. Dark and baking chocolate are the most dangerous.

Baking chocolate and dark chocolate contain more theobromine per gram than milk chocolate. Wrappers, brownie batter, cocoa powder, mocha drinks and sugar-free chocolate that also contains xylitol all count as exposures. Seasoned or filled chocolates add fat and other ingredients but do not make theobromine safer. Cats are small; even a lick is still worth a professional call rather than a wait-and-see experiment.

Plain vs flavored or filled chocolate

Plain dark or baking chocolate is usually higher in theobromine per gram than milk chocolate. Fillings, nuts, coffee flavoring or liqueur add extra hazards but never cancel methylxanthine risk. “White chocolate” is lower in theobromine than dark chocolate yet still unhelpful fat and sugar for cats—and some sugar-free products add xylitol.

What to tell the vet or hotline

Share the product name, cocoa percentage if known, estimated grams eaten, time since exposure, and your cat’s weight. Mention wrappers or batter if those were chewed. Ask the professional whether monitoring at home or clinic care is needed—do not invent a “safe square” of chocolate.

Compared with coffee or xylitol sweets

Coffee and tea are caffeine problems; many sugar-free candies are xylitol problems; chocolate can involve theobromine, caffeine, fat and sometimes xylitol together. When the ingredient list is mixed, bring the package rather than guessing which toxin “matters more.”

What to do

Call your vet or a pet poison hotline right away, even if your cat seems fine. Note the type and amount eaten.
