Can cats eat cooked eggs?

By CatCalc editorial team · Updated 2026-11-12 · Not veterinary advice

Small treat · fully cooked

Fully cooked egg can be a small treat without butter, salt or seasoning.

Why

Fully cooked plain egg can be an occasional food reward, but it is not a required supplement or a complete feline diet. Preparation and total treat calories matter.

Make a separate portion without milk, cheese, butter or seasoning. A whole human breakfast serving should not be copied into a cat's treat allowance.

A cooked egg is an optional food, not a coat supplement

An egg can supply a small food reward without being necessary for a cat that already eats a complete diet. Claims that eggs must be added for a shiny coat or stronger muscles overlook the rest of the ration. Cornell explains that cats need many nutrients in proper amounts and usually do not need supplements alongside balanced food. A visually attractive ingredient does not identify what, if anything, is missing from an individual cat’s diet.

Keep eggs in the role of a limited extra when your veterinarian agrees they are suitable. Do not turn a daily egg into the nutritional center of the bowl, and do not use an egg to repair a homemade diet without professional formulation. The cat-food label guide explains the more useful question: whether the regular food is complete for the cat’s species and life stage.

Fully set is the useful household preparation

For simple sharing, boil an egg until its white and yolk are firm or scramble a small separate amount without additions. FDA advises firm yolks and whites for eggs, with egg dishes cooked to 160°F. Let the portion cool before serving and cut or mash it into manageable pieces. Runny yolk and partly set white should not be presented as equivalent to fully cooked egg.

Raw egg has additional concerns, explained on our raw-egg page. Merck describes avidin in raw egg white binding biotin and notes that cooking reduces antinutritional factors. This is a reason to distinguish preparations, not a claim that one raw lick necessarily causes a deficiency. Heating also needs proper handling: keep raw shells and utensils away from the cooked portion rather than treating cooking as the last possible contamination point.

The breakfast pan can change the ingredient list

Scrambled eggs prepared for people often include milk, cream, cheese, butter, salt or seasoning. A plain-looking curd may therefore be part of a mixed recipe. Make the cat’s portion separately before additions, and avoid scraping a sample from a plate with sauces or garnishes. An omelet containing onion cannot be made suitable by removing the visible onion pieces.

ASPCA identifies dairy as a cause of digestive upset in some pets and Allium ingredients as a cause of red blood cell injury in cats. Compare the milk and onion pages for those separate issues. Egg mayonnaise, quiche, breakfast sandwiches and flavored liquid-egg mixes require their own complete ingredient check. The presence of cooked egg does not certify every food made with it.

Keep the reserved egg portion clearly separate when preparing breakfast for several people. A spoon used to stir seasoned eggs should not become the serving spoon for the plain portion. If somebody else feeds the cat, tell them which container is suitable and how much has already been offered so that a small extra does not become a second breakfast.

A whole egg can be a large extra in a small animal’s day

Do not copy a human serving size into a feline bowl. The relevant quantity is the energy in the portion and its place alongside all other food rewards. CatCalc estimates resting energy as 70 × kilograms to the power 0.75, then applies the chosen life-stage or activity factor. The resulting daily estimate is a starting point, not a prescription, and body condition and clinical needs can change the target.

WSAVA recommends no more than 10% of total daily calories from treats. Use the calorie calculator, the actual preparation’s energy information and a measured portion rather than a rule such as “one egg per cat.” The ceiling includes every other treat as well. It does not establish a validated safe egg dose, and a prescribed diet may require a different reward or none at all.

When to stop the experiment and call the clinic

Introduce no new food while trying to investigate ongoing vomiting or diarrhea without veterinary guidance. If egg is followed by symptoms, write down the amount, recipe, timing and other foods eaten, then stop offering it while seeking advice. A food diary is more useful than repeatedly trying different egg preparations to see which one stays down.

Cornell recommends prompt assessment for vomiting with weakness, lethargy, blood, reduced appetite or accompanying diarrhea. A cat that only wants egg while refusing complete food should also be discussed with the veterinarian. The senior weight and appetite guide is useful for organizing appetite and weight observations. For a suitable healthy cat, a small plain cooked taste can remain an occasional reward; it should not distract from persistent symptoms, a medical feeding plan or a reliable complete daily ration.

How to serve it

Cook until white and yolk are firm, cool, and offer only a small plain portion if it fits the cat's feeding plan.

Treats and complete diets

If this food is appropriate at all, keep it inside the daily calorie budget. Use the calorie calculator and keep treats near or under about 10% of total kcal. Human food is not a balanced cat diet.
