Can cats eat cooked eggs?

By CatCalc editorial team · Updated 2026-11-02 · Not veterinary advice

Small treat · fully cooked

Fully cooked egg can be a small treat without butter, salt or seasoning.

Why

Fully cooked eggs are a good source of protein and amino acids for cats.

Eggs are nutrient-dense; a whole egg may be too many calories for a small cat's treat budget. Share a bite, not a breakfast plate. Butter, milk, cheese, onion powder or chili oil in scrambled eggs reintroduce problems covered on other pages. Raw eggs are covered separately because of pathogen and avidin concerns.

Fully cooked, unseasoned only

Hard-boiled or plain scrambled egg without butter, milk, cheese, salt chili oil or onion powder can be a small treat. Restaurant omelets are not the same food.

Portion and calories

Eggs are calorie-dense. A bite or two is the usual outer bound for a treat budget; a whole egg may crowd out balanced cat food calories for a small cat.

Compared with raw eggs

Raw eggs bring pathogen risk and avidin in raw whites that can interfere with biotin absorption when fed routinely. Prefer fully cooked eggs if you share egg at all.

How to serve it

Scramble or boil with no butter, salt or seasoning.

Treats and complete diets

If this food is appropriate at all, keep it inside the daily calorie budget. Use the calorie calculator and keep treats near or under about 10% of total kcal. Human food is not a balanced cat diet.
