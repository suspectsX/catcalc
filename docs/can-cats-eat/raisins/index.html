Can cats eat raisins?

By CatCalc editorial team · Updated 2026-11-12 · Not veterinary advice

Avoid · limited evidence

Avoid raisins: cat-specific toxicity evidence is limited; consult your vet after ingestion.

If your cat already ate this

  1. Contact your veterinarian or an emergency clinic now—do not wait for symptoms.
  2. In the US you can also call ASPCA Animal Poison Control: 888-426-4435 or Pet Poison Helpline: 855-764-7661 (fees may apply).
  3. Have ready: approximate amount and time, product packaging or ingredients, your cat’s weight, and any symptoms.
  4. Do not induce vomiting or give home remedies unless a veterinarian specifically instructs you to.

Why

Raisins are dried grapes. Kidney injury is well documented in dogs, while feline research remains limited. Do not offer raisins; seek veterinary advice after a cat eats them, and identify other ingredients in the product.

Sultanas and many dried currants used in baking are also grape products. Check the package rather than relying on the color or size of a dried fruit.

Start with the package, not a guessed fruit count

A raisin exposure often begins with a torn bag of trail mix or a missing piece of cake. Keep the packaging and write down how much of the entire product is missing. If you saw only one raisin eaten but the cat had access to an open bag, distinguish the witnessed amount from the possible amount. That detail matters more than making an exact count from incomplete evidence.

The fresh-grape page explains the related feline evidence question. Vet Record's 2026 review found too little cat-specific research for a firm conclusion about toxicity. CatCalc therefore does not publish a safe number of raisins or claim that every exposed cat will develop kidney failure. The appropriate next step is individual veterinary advice, with the product and circumstances clearly described.

Baking does not simplify a mixed-food exposure

Raisin bread, fruitcake and oatmeal cookies are mixtures, so the consultation should cover the recipe as well as the dried fruit. Ask whether chocolate, coffee, alcohol or Allium ingredients were included. Describe sugar-free sweeteners accurately instead of using a single generic label for all of them. Our chocolate guide and xylitol page separate those issues and explain why a canine warning cannot always be pasted onto cats.

Do not assume that the loaf is ordinary bread after visible raisins have been pulled out. The remaining portion cannot be assessed merely from its appearance. When baking at home, keep the recipe and ingredient containers until the food is put away. For a purchased cake, a photograph of the ingredient panel is preferable to a flavor name such as cinnamon cake, which may omit the dried fruit entirely.

Sultanas and currants need accurate names

The word raisin may not be printed on every grape-containing snack. Sultanas and some baking currants are dried grape products. Other fruits also carry the common name currant, making a package label or botanical identification more useful than a remembered name. The WSAVA cat-treat resource specifically groups raisins, currants and sultanas among ingredients to avoid.

When a child or visitor says the cat ate a berry, ask to see the original snack bag. Do not turn an uncertain identification into a confident answer during a phone consultation. Record the brand and product name, and tell the clinician whether a paper cup, plastic wrapper or part of a container may also have been swallowed. A dried-fruit question can involve a second problem from the packaging, so keep those observations separate and report both.

Observation is a clinical plan, not an internet waiting period

The 2026 feline evidence evaluation supports close observation after ingestion, while emphasizing the limits of available research. Ask your veterinarian what follow-up fits this particular cat. A dog-focused kidney-injury timeline is not an established feline schedule, and a normal first meal does not substitute for the plan the clinic recommends.

Report vomiting, refusal of food, unusual tiredness or a change in normal drinking or litter-box habits if they occur. Those signs are not specific enough to diagnose a raisin effect at home. Tell the team whether the cat has existing kidney disease or other health problems, and which medications it takes. If a symptom appears after an earlier call, reconnect with the clinic rather than assuming the first conversation covered every later change. Severe deterioration should be handled as an emergency regardless of the suspected ingredient.

Prevent the repeat accident at the point of access

Dried fruit is easy to leave in several rooms: breakfast cereal at the table, a lunchbox in a backpack, a baking bowl on the counter and a snack bag beside a sofa. Choose closed storage for each location instead of treating the pantry alone as the problem. Clear dropped pieces after serving children and put empty wrappers into a closed bin.

For reward feeding, use the cat's own food or a suitable cat treat rather than sampling from a human snack mix. A tiny calorie contribution is not a safety assessment. The calorie calculator is for feeding estimates, whereas the food-rating guide explains the ingredient decision. Keeping these two questions separate avoids the mistaken idea that a food becomes appropriate just because there is space for it inside a daily energy budget.

What to do

Do not offer raisins. Contact your vet promptly after ingestion with the product and amount.
