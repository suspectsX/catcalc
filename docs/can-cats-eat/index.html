Can Cats Eat…? Human Food Safety List

Search 41 foods for a quick answer.

41 foods

Avoid Do not feed · read the specific risk

  • Chocolate Do not feed · Chocolate contains toxic methylxanthines; contact your vet after ingestion.
  • Onions Do not feed · Onions can damage cats' red blood cells, including cooked or powdered onion.
  • Garlic Do not feed · Garlic can damage red blood cells; avoid it in all forms.
  • Chives Do not feed · Chives and other Alliums can damage cats' red blood cells.
  • Grapes Avoid · limited evidence · Avoid grapes: cat-specific toxicity evidence is limited; consult your vet after ingestion.
  • Raisins Avoid · limited evidence · Avoid raisins: cat-specific toxicity evidence is limited; consult your vet after ingestion.
  • Alcohol Do not feed · Alcohol can cause serious poisoning; ingestion requires urgent veterinary advice.
  • Coffee Do not feed · Caffeine can poison cats; keep coffee, grounds and beans away.
  • Raw Bread Dough Do not feed · Raw yeast dough can expand and produce alcohol in the stomach.
  • Xylitol (Sugar-Free Gum & Sweets) Avoid · limited evidence · Do not feed xylitol products. Cat evidence is limited; do not assume dog toxicity applies.
  • Cooked Bones Do not feed · Do not feed cooked bones: they can splinter, choke or injure a cat.
  • Avocado Avoid · pit and fat · Avoid feeding avocado; the pit and fat pose risks, and cat-specific persin evidence is limited.

Caution Preparation and dietary limits apply

  • Milk Caution · lactose · Cow's milk often causes digestive upset; it is not a kitten milk replacement.
  • Cheese Caution · fat and salt · Only a rare tiny piece if tolerated; cheese may cause digestive upset.
  • Yogurt Caution · plain only · A small amount of plain unsweetened yogurt may be tolerated; check ingredients.
  • Tuna Caution · treat only · Plain tuna in spring water is an occasional treat, never a complete diet.
  • Raw Fish Cook first · Do not serve raw fish. Cook it plainly and remove bones before sharing.
  • Raw Eggs Cook first · Do not serve raw eggs. Cook eggs fully without seasoning before sharing.
  • Dog Food Not a cat diet · An accidental bite is usually not harmful, but dog food cannot replace a complete cat diet.
  • Ham & Bacon Caution · salt and fat · Avoid routine feeding of ham or bacon; a tiny bite is unlikely to harm most healthy cats.
  • Peanut Butter Caution · ingredients · At most a lick of xylitol-free peanut butter; it is sticky and high in fat.
  • Bread Caution · plain baked only · Only a small piece of plain baked bread; avoid raw dough, garlic and raisin breads.
  • Banana Caution · tiny portion · A very small peeled banana piece can be an occasional treat, not a meal.
  • Apples Caution · flesh only · Only a small piece of apple flesh with the core, seeds and stems removed.

Small treats Plain preparation and small portions

  • Cooked Chicken Small treat · cooked · Plain cooked chicken can be a small treat; remove skin and bones.
  • Cooked Turkey Small treat · cooked · Plain cooked turkey can be a small treat without skin, gravy, stuffing or bones.
  • Cooked Salmon Small treat · cooked · Offer only a small amount of plain cooked salmon with all bones removed.
  • Cooked Eggs Small treat · fully cooked · Fully cooked egg can be a small treat without butter, salt or seasoning.
  • Cooked Shrimp Small treat · cooked · Plain cooked shrimp can be a small treat with the shell and tail removed.
  • Pumpkin Small treat · plain · Offer a small amount of plain pumpkin, not sweetened or spiced pie filling.
  • Blueberries Small treat · Offer only one or two blueberries, cut in half for small cats.
  • Watermelon Small treat · flesh only · Offer a small watermelon cube with all seeds and rind removed.
  • Strawberries Small treat · Offer only a small strawberry piece with the leaves removed.
  • Cantaloupe Small treat · flesh only · Offer a small cantaloupe piece with rind and seeds removed.
  • Carrots Small treat · cooked · Offer soft cooked carrot cut into small pieces; raw chunks can cause choking.
  • Cucumber Small treat · Offer a small amount of peeled cucumber sliced thinly.
  • Green Beans Small treat · cooked · Offer a little plain cooked green bean without salt, butter or garlic.
  • Peas Small treat · plain · Offer a small amount of plain cooked or thawed peas without seasoning.
  • Rice Small treat · cooked · Only a little plain cooked rice; it cannot replace a complete cat diet.
  • Oatmeal Small treat · water-cooked · Offer a little plain oatmeal cooked with water, not milk or sweeteners.
  • Catnip Moderation · Offer a small amount of catnip; eating too much can cause vomiting or diarrhea.

Cats are obligate carnivores: they need nutrients found in animal tissue. Treats, including human food, should stay under about 10% of daily calories.

CatCalc provides general information only and is not a substitute for veterinary advice. If you think your cat ate something toxic, call your vet, the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center (888-426-4435, fee may apply) or Pet Poison Helpline (855-764-7661).
