Can cats eat catnip?

By CatCalc editorial team · Updated 2026-11-12 · Not veterinary advice

Caution · digestive effects

Catnip may cause vomiting or diarrhea; use supervised enrichment and avoid large ingestion.

Why

Catnip can be used for enrichment, but it can also cause vomiting, diarrhea, stimulation or sedation. ASPCA classifies the plant as toxic to cats; this is not a reason to treat every small toy exposure as a severe poisoning.

Catnip is optional enrichment rather than food or medicine. Different preparations and toy materials need separate assessment, and no response needs to be forced.

Why a familiar toy ingredient still deserves a caution label

Catnip is widely used for enrichment, but describing it as unconditionally non-toxic hides an important qualification. The ASPCA entry for Nepeta cataria lists nepetalactone and warns of vomiting, diarrhea and either stimulation or sedation in cats. Our caution is about those possible effects and the way the product is used. It is not a claim that a catnip toy has the same risk or mechanism as a highly dangerous plant such as a true lily.

Read the lily guide for that separate emergency. Plant names and risk levels should not be blended together. Catnip also is not a dietary requirement, so a cat that does not respond has nothing missing from its meals. There is no reason to increase exposure repeatedly to make every cat perform the same rolling or playful behavior seen in a video.

Smelling a toy and eating a pile are different exposures

Choose a simple, identifiable product and read its directions before offering it. A small catnip-containing toy, loose dried herb, fresh leaves and an extract do not provide the same route or concentration of exposure. Do not use a familiar name to assume that a bottle of concentrated liquid can be poured on food or applied directly to the cat. Ask the veterinary team about an unfamiliar preparation instead of making that conversion yourself.

For a first enrichment session, keep the product contained and stay nearby. Let the cat approach or leave voluntarily. Put loose supplies away afterward, where the cat cannot tear open the packet and eat a large amount without you noticing. This is practical exposure control, not a published safe-dose schedule. We do not give a grams-per-kilogram ingestion allowance because this page does not have evidence that would justify one for every formulation.

Inspect the toy, not only the herb inside it

A damaged catnip toy may introduce a problem unrelated to the plant. Check seams, ribbons, string, detachable decorations and exposed stuffing before play and after rough use. Remove a damaged item rather than leaving it out because the cat still seems interested. Keep the label or a photograph so you can describe its contents if something goes missing.

Merck's swallowed-object guidance describes swallowed foreign objects as a separate clinical concern. If string or part of a toy may have been swallowed, contact a veterinarian even if you initially noticed the incident because of catnip. Do not give more food as an attempt to push it through or pull at material protruding from the mouth or anus. A product's pleasant effect does not make its fabric, plastic or cord edible. Supervision remains useful even for an item marketed specifically to cats.

Observe the individual cat without chasing a stronger response

A quiet response, excited response or lack of interest can all be reasons to end a short session on the cat's terms. Keep enough space that an aroused cat is not forced into close contact with another pet. If play becomes uncomfortable or the cat wants to withdraw, take a break rather than adding more herb. Choose an ordinary toy or a different interaction next time if catnip does not suit the household.

Do not use catnip to conceal an ongoing behavioral or medical change. A cat hiding more, eating less or changing its litter-box habits needs attention to that change; a brief response to a toy does not rule out illness. The senior appetite guide can help structure observations. Enrichment is valuable as an activity, but it is not a diagnosis or a replacement for an appropriate care plan.

After ingestion or an unexpected reaction

If a cat eats loose herb and vomits or develops diarrhea, remove the product and contact your veterinarian for advice about the amount, timing and signs. Keep the packaging and mention extracts, sprays or additional ingredients. Severe distress, breathing difficulty, collapse or suspected toy ingestion needs urgent attention rather than an assumption that the response is a normal catnip episode.

The rating method explains why this site distinguishes toxicity, physical injury and uncertainty. Catnip does not need a calorie allowance calculated like a cooked-chicken treat, and it should not be added to meals to fill a snack budget. If eating or weight is the concern, use the calorie calculator only for its stated planning role and discuss the actual problem with the clinic. Keep enrichment products and nutritional decisions separate.

Use for supervised enrichment

Use an identifiable enrichment product as directed and supervise it. Remove it after an adverse reaction; ask your veterinarian about ingestion, concentrated products or swallowed toy parts.

Treats and complete diets

If this food is appropriate at all, keep it inside the daily calorie budget. Use the calorie calculator and keep treats near or under about 10% of total kcal. Human food is not a balanced cat diet.
