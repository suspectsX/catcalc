Can cats eat catnip?

By CatCalc editorial team · Updated 2026-11-02 · Not veterinary advice

Moderation

Offer a small amount of catnip; eating too much can cause vomiting or diarrhea.

Why

Catnip is non-toxic. About half to two-thirds of cats respond to it with playful behavior.

Catnip (Nepeta cataria) is non-toxic for cats. Roughly half to two-thirds of cats respond with playful behavior; response is partly genetic. Eating a large pile may cause vomiting or diarrhea. Toys should be sized so the cat cannot swallow string, plastic eyes or loose stuffing.

How cats respond

Catnip is non-toxic. About half to two-thirds of cats show playful or rolling responses; others shrug. Response has a genetic component, so indifference is normal.

Toys and ingestion amounts

Offer a small pinch or a well-made toy. Eating a large pile may cause vomiting or diarrhea. Avoid toys with loose string, plastic eyes or stuffing the cat can swallow.

Not a medical treatment

Catnip is enrichment, not a cure for anxiety, pain or litter-box disease. Persistent behavior or health changes deserve a veterinary visit.

How to offer it

If a cat eats a lot, it may vomit or have diarrhea; offer in moderation.

Treats and complete diets

If this food is appropriate at all, keep it inside the daily calorie budget. Use the calorie calculator and keep treats near or under about 10% of total kcal. Human food is not a balanced cat diet.
