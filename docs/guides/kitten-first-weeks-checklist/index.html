New Kitten Checklist: Feeding, Safety, and Vet Timing

A practical first-weeks checklist for new kitten guardians: diet stage, household hazards, litter basics, and when to schedule veterinary care.

By CatCalc editorial team · Updated 2026-11-02. General information only—not veterinary advice. We are not veterinarians.

How to use this checklist

Print this page or copy the boxes into a notes app. Check items as you go. Timing for vaccines and tests is set by your clinic—use those rows as prompts to ask, not as a universal medical schedule. Life-stage context: AAHA/AAFP feline life stage definitions and AAFP/AAHA life stage guidelines overview.

Before arrival (checkable)

  • ☐ Quiet room ready: litter box, food and water spaced apart, scratcher, hiding spot
  • ☐ True lilies and daylilies removed from the home (lily guide)
  • ☐ Cords protected; windows secured; washer/dryer doors kept closed
  • ☐ Carrier lined with a towel; previous diet brand written down for a gradual transition
  • ☐ Appointment request sent to a feline-friendly clinic for an intake exam
  • ☐ Household agreement on who feeds, scoops, and child/dog supervision rules

First week at home (checkable)

  • ☐ Growth or all-life-stages complete food in use (not an adult “light” diet unless a vet directs)
  • ☐ Meals measured so appetite changes are visible; calorie calculator used only as a ballpark with the kitten factor
  • ☐ Cow’s milk avoided; orphans on commercial kitten milk replacer only with veterinary instructions (milk page)
  • ☐ Litter scooped daily; note stool quality each day
  • ☐ Short wand-toy play sessions; hands not used as toys
  • ☐ Weight recorded every 2–3 days on the same scale
  • ☐ String, rubber bands, hair ties, and toxic human foods secured (food list)
  • ☐ Visitors limited if the kitten is fearful; dog introductions postponed until a plan exists

First veterinary visit — bring / ask (checkable)

  • ☐ Previous medical records, vaccine history, and deworming dates
  • ☐ Current food name, feeding amounts, and treat list
  • ☐ Home weight log and stool notes from week one
  • ☐ Ask: parasite plan, vaccine schedule for this kitten’s age/region, FeLV/FIV testing discussion
  • ☐ Ask: microchip, spay/neuter timing, and recovery plan after surgery
  • ☐ Ask: diet transition if changing brands; bring a fresh stool sample if requested

Your clinic sets the exact schedule. This list only helps you arrive prepared.

FAQ

How soon should a new kitten see a vet?

Soon after arrival is typical so parasites, vaccines, and congenital issues can be checked. Ask your clinic for an appointment based on the kitten's age and history.

Can I feed an adult cat food to a kitten to save money?

Growth diets are formulated differently. Use a kitten or all-life-stages complete food unless your veterinarian directs otherwise.

How do I estimate kitten calories?

Use the calculator's kitten factor as a starting point, then adjust with frequent weigh-ins and body-condition checks guided by your vet.
