The formula (RER × factor)

Veterinary nutrition starts from the Resting Energy Requirement:

RER = 70 × (body weight in kg)0.75

RER estimates calories needed at rest. Daily needs are then approximated by multiplying RER by a life-stage or activity factor. CatCalc exposes common teaching factors such as about 1.2 for many neutered adults, 1.4 for intact adults, 1.0 for inactive adults, 0.8 for supervised weight loss, and a higher factor for kittens under one year. These are starting points used in veterinary nutrition education, not a prescription for your individual cat.

Context: AAHA nutrition and weight management guidelines. Treats count inside the total budget. Ill, pregnant, nursing or underweight cats need hands-on veterinary plans.

Worked examples (same math as the calculator)

The live tool uses RER = 70 × kg0.75, then multiplies by the selected factor, and rounds displayed kcal with ordinary rounding. Food amounts use the optional kcal field (kcal/kg, kcal/cup, or kcal/can).

  1. 4.5 kg · neutered/spayed (factor 1.2) · 80 kcal/can: RER = 70 × 4.50.75 ≈ 216.28 kcal (tool shows RER 216). Daily estimate ≈ 216.28 × 1.2 ≈ 259.53 → tool shows 260 kcal. Food-only allowance ≈ 259.53 ÷ 80 ≈ 3.24 cans before treats. Treat budget inside that total ≈ 10% ≈ 26 kcal.
  2. 10 lb · inactive adult (factor 1.0), no food field: 10 lb × 0.45359237 ≈ 4.54 kg. RER ≈ 217.57 → tool shows RER 218. Daily estimate ≈ 218 kcal (factor 1.0). Leaving food calories blank skips the portion line.
  3. 3.0 kg · kitten under 1 year (factor 2.5) · 400 kcal/cup: RER ≈ 159.57 → tool shows 160. Daily ≈ 159.57 × 2.5 ≈ 398.92 → tool shows 399 kcal. Cups ≈ 398.92 ÷ 400 ≈ 1.00 cup before treats.

Swap in your cat’s real weight and the kcal statement from your bag or can. Wet and dry foods differ wildly in energy density; never copy a neighbor’s cup count. Weights outside 0.3–15 kg (about 0.66–33.07 lb), empty required fields, or non-numbers show an inline error instead of a guess.

How to use the optional food field

Enter kcal/kg if you weigh food on a kitchen scale, or kcal/cup / kcal/can when the manufacturer prints those figures. Our label-reading guide shows where those numbers usually appear. If the field is blank, you still get a calorie target to take to the clinic or compare with the feeding directions.

Body condition beats any app

Two cats at the same scale weight can need different calories if one is lean muscle and the other is overweight. Feel for ribs under a slight fat cover and look for a waist from above. Recheck weight every 2–4 weeks when adjusting portions. Unexplained loss in a senior is a reason to book a vet visit—see the senior appetite guide.

FAQ

How many calories does a cat need per day?

A typical 4.5 kg neutered adult lands near about 260 kcal/day with the 1.2 factor on this tool (RER ≈ 216). Needs vary. The calculator uses RER = 70 × (weight in kg)^0.75 multiplied by a life-stage factor.

How much wet or dry food should I feed my cat?

Divide your cat's daily calorie needs by the calories in the food (printed on the bag or can as kcal/kg, kcal/cup or kcal/can).

How many treats can a cat have?

Keep treats under about 10% of the total daily calorie budget. Subtract treat calories from the food allowance rather than adding them on top.

Should I use ideal weight or current weight for weight loss?

Veterinary weight-loss plans usually target feeding for an agreed ideal or stepwise target weight. Ask your vet before using the weight-loss factor on a very overweight or ill cat.

Why does my bag list kcal/kg instead of kcal/cup?

Manufacturers may publish energy density by weight. Convert with a kitchen scale, or use the per-cup or per-can figure when the label provides one.
